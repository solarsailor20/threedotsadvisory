The approach

How I invest.

Over long periods, markets have rewarded patience and diversification, and taxes have penalized frequent trading. I build portfolios around both facts, and I stick to the rules when markets make that hard.

The core model

Designed around after-tax returns.

Most portfolios are built first and taxed later. Core & Overlay, the model I use for most taxable accounts, starts with the tax bill. It keeps the long-term holdings separate from the positions used to manage risk.

The core

Long-term holdings, rarely sold

Broad U.S. stocks and physical gold, held for years. Because I almost never sell it, gains go untaxed and keep compounding.

The reserve

Cash and Treasury bills

Backs the futures positions and pays the yearly tax bill, so I don’t have to sell the core to raise cash.

The overlay

Where risk is managed

Exchange-traded futures add exposure to stocks and Treasuries when their trends are up, plus a trend-following position across several markets. When trends turn down, I cut the futures and leave the core alone.

Core & Overlay is for taxable accounts. IRAs and other tax-advantaged accounts use a related model.

Four principles

  1. 01

    Taxes come first

    The holdings that carry most of the risk are the ones I sell least. I harvest losses when they appear, and when I do trim, I sell long-term lots first.

  2. 02

    Cutting risk without a tax bill

    Futures are taxed every year whether or not they’re sold, so closing them in a downturn doesn’t create an extra tax bill. I can reduce exposure quickly and leave the core alone.

  3. 03

    Rules set in advance

    Trend and volatility signals, checked on a fixed schedule, decide when exposure goes up or down. I don’t forecast markets or override the rules on instinct.

  4. 04

    Several sources of return

    U.S. stocks, gold, Treasuries and trend-following have tended to do well in different years, so the portfolio doesn’t depend on any one market.

Risks

What can go wrong.

It uses leverage

When trends are up, total stock exposure can be well above 100% of the account. Leverage makes losses bigger as well as gains, and a one-day crash can happen before any rule reacts.

It won’t track the S&P 500

Some years it will trail the index, occasionally several years in a row.

Signals can be late

Signals are checked on a schedule, so they can miss sharp reversals. They’re meant to reduce losses in long declines, and they won’t prevent them.
